Neurodiversity

Support without stigma

Every child’s brain is different. We share practical strategies that support attention, sensory needs, anxiety, and social communication — in respectful, strengths-based ways.

This content is educational. It is not medical advice and does not replace professional assessment or therapy. If you have safety concerns or urgent needs, contact local services immediately.

ADHD support (home & school)

Use short instructions, visual reminders, predictable routines, and movement breaks.

Try: 1-step directions, timers, “first-then” language, reward charts for effort.

Anxiety & overwhelm

Teach calming skills before stressful moments. Reduce uncertainty with previewing and choices.

Try: breathing, body scan, “what happens next” plan, safe coping corner.

Sensory needs

Some children need sensory input to feel regulated and focused.

Try: heavy-work play, fidgets, quiet headphones, sensory breaks.

Working with professionals (OT / SLP / therapy)

Occupational Therapy (OT): sensory regulation, fine motor skills, daily living routines.

Speech-Language Pathology (SLP): communication, social language, articulation.

Behavioral supports: understanding triggers, building skills, reinforcing progress.

Tip: Ask for “home strategies” and practice them consistently for 2–4 weeks.
