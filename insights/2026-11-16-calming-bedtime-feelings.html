Calming Big Bedtime Feelings

Simple routines help children settle their nervous systems at night.

An illustrative, composite scenario — not a real named incident. Educational content only; not medical or legal advice. For diagnosis or support, consult qualified professionals.

"Lina" resists going to bed, feeling anxious and upset, making the whole family tense in the evening.

Despite trying to distract her with stories and songs, "Lina"’s big emotions keep surfacing, leading to tears and restlessness.

Behaviour is communication

Instead of pushing through the resistance, consider how the environment and routines can support "Lina"’s sense of safety and ability to calm down.

What was likely really going on

⏳

The Nervous System Is Overwhelmed

Children can feel overwhelmed by the transition to bedtime after a busy day, leading to anxiety and big feelings.

🔊

Need for Security and Predictability

Bedtime routines offer comfort by signaling safety and helping children anticipate what comes next.

🧠

Difficulty Expressing Emotions

Young children often struggle to name or manage their feelings, so anxiety can show up as resistance or tears.

What could have helped

🎭

Reggio Emilia: Create a Soothing Space

Design a calming bedtime environment with soft lighting and familiar objects that help children feel secure and capable of settling themselves.

🧺

Montessori: Offer Freedom Within Boundaries

Provide choices like selecting pajamas or a bedtime story to give children a sense of control, making the routine feel respectful and steady.

💛

Early-childhood practice: Regulate First, Teach Second

Focus first on comforting your child with gentle touch or slow breathing exercises before guiding them toward sleep.

Try this at home & school

  • Dim the lights 30 minutes before bed to signal winding down.
  • Let your child choose a stuffed animal or blanket for comfort.
  • Use a consistent, calming phrase when starting the bedtime routine.
  • Practice slow, deep breaths together to soothe nerves.
  • Keep a favorite book nearby for a quiet moment of connection.
